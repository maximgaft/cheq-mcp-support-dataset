Generated by `evals/check_labels.py`. Offline, no API key: it compares the committed `data/answer_labels.parquet` (Claude Haiku 4.5, 39,739 answers, $9.53) against the 40 tickets in `evals/answer_labels.yaml` that I read and labelled by hand.

| | agreement |
|---|---:|
| `kind`, four-way | **28/40** (70%) |
| `ask_is_specific`, on information requests | 12/16 (75%) |

## Where the four-way fails

| mine ↓ / model → | `acknowledgement` | `information_request` | `not_a_support_reply` | `resolution` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `acknowledgement` | 6 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| `information_request` | 0 | 16 | 0 | 0 |
| `not_a_support_reply` | 1 | 2 | 2 | 0 |
| `resolution` | 0 | 1 | 1 | 4 |

`information_request` is recalled perfectly; `acknowledgement` is where the model scatters. Corpus-wide it calls 62% information requests where the adjudicated sample says 40%, so it over-calls the class it never misses.

## Why the collapsed view is still usable

That confusion moves tickets *within* `dead_end` — a vague ask and a bare acknowledgement are both dead ends — so the dominant error cancels at the level the business claim uses. Two independent estimates:

| state | model, 39,739 | adjudicated, n=40 |
|---|---:|---:|
| `dead_end` | 49.8% | 55.0% |
| `actionable_ask` | 37.8% | 30.0% |
| `resolved` | 12.4% | 15.0% |

**About half of all first replies are dead ends** — they neither resolve the ticket nor tell the customer what to send, so each one costs a round trip and buys nothing. The four-way labels should not be quoted on their own at 70% agreement; this collapse is what the design note reports.

Caveat: n=40 adjudicated tickets. The intervals are wide, which is why the two estimates agreeing to within a few points is the claim, not either number alone.

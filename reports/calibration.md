# Similarity floor calibration

Generated by `pipeline/07_calibrate.py`. Model `intfloat/multilingual-e5-small`, centred vectors.

## Populations

| population | n | mean | min | max |
|------------|--:|-----:|----:|----:|
| real tickets (generated) | 400 | 0.660 | 0.410 | 0.896 |
| handwritten, on topic | 15 | 0.497 | 0.402 | 0.607 |
| handwritten, off topic | 20 | 0.348 | 0.278 | 0.413 |

**Chosen floor: 0.45** - midpoint of the off-topic ceiling (0.413) and the real-ticket 5th percentile (0.495).

## Two effects of the same size

The floor does separate topic: handwritten on-topic queries score +0.149 above handwritten off-topic ones. But generated text also scores +0.163 above handwritten text *regardless of topic*. Those two effects are comparable in magnitude, and a single threshold on similarity cannot tell them apart.

So the floor is right for the input shape it was calibrated on, and wrong for any other. On real tickets - the production shape, where a webhook delivers an actual ticket - it accepts 98% and rejects 100% of off-topic queries. Applied to a hand-typed question it wrongly rejects **33%** of legitimate on-topic ones.

`find_similar_tickets` therefore documents that `mode="precedent"` expects ticket text, and offers `mode="explore"`, which applies no floor, for queries typed by a person.

**The two populations genuinely overlap**, so no floor satisfies both: accepting every on-topic query needs a floor at or below 0.402, and rejecting every off-topic one needs it above 0.413. 0.40 is the visible alternative - it accepts 100% of both legitimate populations and lets through 1 of 20 off-topic queries. 0.45 is the conservative end of that trade, chosen because a confidently wrong draft costs more than a refusal. Which end to sit at is a decision for whoever owns the support queue, not a property of the data.

A related symptom: a bare corpus greeting with no content in it at all ("Dear Customer Support Team, I hope this message finds you well.") scores 0.468 and clears the floor, because provenance alone carries it over the line.

## Caveats

- The off-topic column is measured on 20 handwritten queries. A 0% cell means zero of 20, which bounds the true rate only loosely (95% upper bound around 14%).
- The on-topic set is 15 cases, and its minimum (0.402) overlaps the off-topic maximum (0.413). No separate handwritten floor is fitted here - that would be fitting a threshold to a dozen points.
- The corpus is ~40k synthetic tickets from one generator, so it is topically narrow and genuine no-precedent cases are rarer here than against a real archive.

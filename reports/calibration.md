## Held-out check

The floor above was set using the 20 off-topic and 15 on-topic queries in `evals/abstention_queries.yaml`, so its figures on them are in-sample. `evals/abstention_holdout.yaml` holds 25 off-topic and 15 on-topic typed queries drafted with an AI assistant after the floor was fixed, reviewed by hand, and never used for fitting; the off-topic half includes five fault reports in domains the corpus does not cover, the shape the fitting set treats as its hard cases. At the same floor:

| held-out population | n | mean | max | at the floor |
|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| off topic | 25 | 0.358 | 0.509 | 24 of 25 rejected |
| on topic, typed | 15 | 0.431 | 0.598 | 6 of 15 accepted |

Out of sample, the floor rejects 24 of 25 off-topic queries and accepts 6 of 15 on-topic typed questions, against 9 of 15 on the fitting set. The on-topic figures never enter the floor rule (it uses only the off-topic ceiling and the corpus percentile), so the difference is sampling variation plus a shorter, more generic held-out set: with n=15 the 95% interval on 6 of 15 runs roughly 15-65%. Pooled over all 30 typed on-topic questions the floor accepts 15. With n=25 the off-topic rejection rate is still only loosely bounded, but it is no longer the number the floor was chosen on.

## Two effects of the same size

The floor does separate topic: handwritten on-topic queries score +0.134 above handwritten off-topic ones. But generated text also scores +0.182 above handwritten text *regardless of topic*. Those two effects are comparable in magnitude, and a single threshold on similarity cannot tell them apart. Roughly 50% of the second effect is plausibly length: the same 400 tickets cut to the handwritten queries' median length (12 words) score 0.582. Truncation also drops content and leaves fragments, so that is a rough attribution rather than a clean length effect; style, and whether a paraphrase of the ticket already sits in the index, are not separated here.

So the floor is right for the input shape it was calibrated on, and wrong for any other. On corpus tickets - the shape a webhook delivers - it accepts 97.8% and rejects 20 of the 20 off-topic queries it was fitted against. Applied to a hand-typed question it wrongly rejects **40%** of legitimate on-topic ones.

`find_similar_tickets` therefore applies the floor to `has_precedent` but returns the hits either way, and its guidance says that for a question typed by a person the floor is advisory rather than a refusal.

**The two populations genuinely overlap**, so no floor satisfies both: accepting every on-topic query needs a floor at or below 0.379, and rejecting every off-topic one needs it above 0.441. 0.40 is the visible alternative: it accepts 100% of corpus tickets and 87% of handwritten on-topic questions, and lets through 2 of 20 off-topic queries. 0.47 is the conservative end of that trade, chosen because a confidently wrong draft costs more than a refusal. Which end to sit at is a decision for whoever owns the support queue, not a property of the data.

A related symptom: a bare corpus greeting with no content in it at all ("Dear Customer Support Team, I hope this message finds you well.") scores 0.434 - just below the 0.47 floor. Provenance alone carries a content-free string that close to the line.

## Caveats

- The off-topic column is measured on the same 20 handwritten queries the floor was fitted against, so its 0% at the floor is in-sample. Zero of 20 bounds the true rate only loosely (95% upper bound around 14%).
- The on-topic set is 15 cases, and its minimum (0.379) overlaps the off-topic maximum (0.441). No separate handwritten floor is fitted here - that would be fitting a threshold to a dozen points.
- The corpus is ~40k synthetic tickets from one generator, so it is topically narrow and genuine no-precedent cases are rarer here than against a real archive.

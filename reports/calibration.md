## Two effects of the same size

The floor does separate topic: handwritten on-topic queries score +0.134 above handwritten off-topic ones. But generated text also scores +0.182 above handwritten text *regardless of topic*. Those two effects are comparable in magnitude, and a single threshold on similarity cannot tell them apart. Roughly 50% of the second effect is plausibly length: the same 400 tickets cut to the handwritten queries' median length (12 words) score 0.582. Truncation also drops content and leaves fragments, so that is a rough attribution rather than a clean length effect; style, and whether a paraphrase of the ticket already sits in the index, are not separated here.

So the floor is right for the input shape it was calibrated on, and wrong for any other. On real tickets - the production shape, where a webhook delivers an actual ticket - it accepts 97.8% and rejects 100% of off-topic queries. Applied to a hand-typed question it wrongly rejects **40%** of legitimate on-topic ones.

`find_similar_tickets` therefore applies the floor to `has_precedent` but returns the hits either way, and its guidance says that for a question typed by a person the floor is advisory rather than a refusal.

**The two populations genuinely overlap**, so no floor satisfies both: accepting every on-topic query needs a floor at or below 0.379, and rejecting every off-topic one needs it above 0.441. 0.40 is the visible alternative - it accepts 100% of both legitimate populations and lets through 1 of 20 off-topic queries. 0.47 is the conservative end of that trade, chosen because a confidently wrong draft costs more than a refusal. Which end to sit at is a decision for whoever owns the support queue, not a property of the data.

A related symptom: a bare corpus greeting with no content in it at all ("Dear Customer Support Team, I hope this message finds you well.") scores 0.434 - just below the 0.47 floor. Provenance alone carries a content-free string that close to the line.

## Caveats

- The off-topic column is measured on 20 handwritten queries. A 0% cell means zero of 20, which bounds the true rate only loosely (95% upper bound around 14%).
- The on-topic set is 15 cases, and its minimum (0.379) overlaps the off-topic maximum (0.441). No separate handwritten floor is fitted here - that would be fitting a threshold to a dozen points.
- The corpus is ~40k synthetic tickets from one generator, so it is topically narrow and genuine no-precedent cases are rarer here than against a real archive.

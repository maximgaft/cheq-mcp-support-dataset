## Two confidence signals, and when to trust which

Both are written to `routing_metrics.json` and read by `suggest_routing`.

**Top-1 similarity** is the better predictor - AUC 0.762 against 0.642 for agreement. Combining them naively is worse than either, so they are reported separately rather than blended.

| top-1 similarity | tickets | accuracy |
|------------------|--------:|---------:|
| >= 0.80 | 377 | 0.981 |
| >= 0.70 | 1,459 | 0.866 |
| >= 0.60 | 1,329 | 0.673 |
| >= 0.00 | 843 | 0.452 |

**Agreement@3** - the share of the top 3 neighbours that share the nearest one's queue - predicts less well but stays meaningful.

| agreement@3 | tickets | accuracy |
|------------|--------:|---------:|
| >= 1.0 | 586 | 0.903 |
| >= 0.6 | 1,404 | 0.790 |
| >= 0.0 | 2,018 | 0.629 |

### Why both

Similarity is better within a fixed input shape and *not comparable across shapes*. Handwritten text scores lower than this corpus's generated text whatever its topic, so a similarity band measured on tickets misreads a hand-typed question as low-confidence when it is not. Agreement does not move that way:

| signal | generated tickets | handwritten queries | change |
|--------|------------------:|--------------------:|-------:|
| similarity | 0.680 | 0.492 | -28% |
| agreement@3 | 0.548 | 0.644 | +18% |

Near-duplicate hits are collapsed before agreement is counted (doc-doc similarity 0.85), so "three neighbours agree" means three distinct tickets. That costs predictive power - agreement AUC falls from 0.684 to 0.642 - because duplicated neighbours also signal a dense, well-covered region, which correlates with being right. The uncollapsed signal was the better predictor on this corpus by partly measuring an artifact that better deduplication would remove, so the weaker honest signal is the one published.

So `suggest_routing` returns both. When they disagree the input is probably not ticket-shaped, and agreement is the one to believe - a hand-typed ticket that scored 0.514 similarity (a 49% band) had four of five neighbours agreeing, and the prediction was right.

Near-duplicate matching contributes little: only 9.4% of test tickets have a neighbour above 0.80, and excluding all of them macro-F1 is 0.656 against 0.691 overall. Exact-duplicate removal was necessary but this corpus also contains paraphrase pairs, so a similarity-based dedup would be the next step on a real archive.

## Where the errors are

1,100 of 4,008 test tickets are misrouted. 325 of those errors (30%) are *within* Technical Support / IT Support / Product Support - three queues whose tickets overlap semantically, and which a human triager would also confuse. The ceiling here is label ambiguity, not model capacity.
